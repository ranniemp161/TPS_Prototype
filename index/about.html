Who is caring
for the mother?

The Postpartum Suite began with a simple question: who is caring for the mother?

After Ami had her second baby, Aminat wanted to give her something that would genuinely support her recovery. Not another gift for the baby, but something centred on her.

So, Aminat arranged postpartum support at home for her sister.

Meals were prepared, she had support with the baby, and the everyday demands of the home no longer rested entirely on Aminat while she recovered.

What stayed with us was not one particular meal, treatment or task. It was the difference that came from having someone else take responsibility for some of the things she would ordinarily have had to manage herself.

She didn't have to organise every meal, think ahead to the next household task, or constantly consider what needed doing next. For a time, she could step away from managing everything around her and focus on her recovery.

That was the beginning of The Postpartum Suite.

Once we saw the difference thoughtful, practical support could make, it became hard to understand why this kind of care wasn't considered a more normal part of postpartum recovery.

So much thought goes into preparing for a baby. We began to ask why the same consideration was not given to how the mother would be cared for once the baby arrived.

That question shaped everything that followed.

The care we felt was missing

We didn't want to create a service that put another pair of hands in the home. We wanted to create something more considered: care built around what a woman may genuinely need after birth.

That might mean time to recover, nourishing food prepared for her, postpartum treatments that give attention to her body and wellbeing, someone she trusts with her baby, and a home that continues to function without depending entirely on her.

  • Time to recover
  • Nourishing food prepared for her
  • Postpartum treatments
  • Someone she trusts with her baby
  • A home that continues to function

It also means having the space to sleep, shower, eat and adjust to life with a new baby without continually thinking about what needs to happen next.

Our role is to ease some of those practical demands, creating more space for the mother to recover.

That principle sits at the heart of TPS.

Our founders

Two sisters. Two perspectives. One shared standard.

Our different backgrounds and the perspectives each of us brings have shaped The Postpartum Suite.

Aminat Sowunmi

Co-Founder

Aminat brings more than a decade of healthcare experience, along with a background in public health and mental health.

Her career has informed many of TPS's standards, from safety and professional boundaries to dignity, attentiveness, and the experience of receiving care during a vulnerable time in life.

Her experience across physical and mental health has also shaped her understanding of the emotional adjustment that can accompany the postpartum period, and the importance of care that feels calm, respectful and responsive to the individual.

Years spent caring for people have reinforced something that remains central to TPS: good care is not simply about completing a task well. It is also about how someone feels while receiving it.

We want mothers to feel safe, respected, comfortable and genuinely cared for.

That perspective shapes how we train our practitioners, how we approach care in the home, and the standards we expect throughout each family’s experience.

Ami Olowu

Co-Founder

Ami’s vision for The Postpartum Suite is shaped by both lived experience and more than a decade of experience in technology, product management and digital transformation. As a mother, she understands the importance of practical help, clear information and compassionate support during the postpartum period. Her own experience showed her how meaningful care can be when it is personal, well organised and responsive to what families are truly navigating.

As a senior product manager, Ami is experienced in understanding complex needs, identifying gaps and turning ideas into effective solutions. She brings this same thoughtful, client-centred approach to The Postpartum Suite, listening closely to families, designing considered programmes and creating a service that feels both supportive and personal.

Both co-founders are building a service that helps mothers feel supported, understood and cared for during a significant transition in their lives.

Inspired by traditions that have long centred the mother

The idea of protecting a woman’s recovery after birth is not new.

Across cultures and generations, the postpartum period has long been recognised as a distinct time of recovery, a time when mothers are nourished, rest is protected, the body is cared for, and practical responsibilities are shared.

We were particularly drawn to the thoughtfulness of Malaysian confinement care, alongside postpartum traditions from other cultures that treat a mother’s recovery as an important part of the postpartum period.

At the heart of these traditions is a principle that resonated deeply with us:

Birth is not where care for the mother ends. It is where another kind of care begins.

The Postpartum Suite draws on these traditions while creating care that fits into the lives of modern families.

We do not prescribe one version of postpartum recovery. Every mother is different, every family is different, and every home has its own rhythm.

Our role is to understand what matters to each mother and shape her care around it.

The same intention, in every home

The Postpartum Suite began because one sister wanted another to feel cared for after birth.

That intention has never changed.

Today, it informs every part of TPS, from the programmes we design and the practitioners we train to the meals prepared, treatments delivered and hours of rest we help protect.

After months of preparing for a baby’s arrival, we believe the mother’s recovery deserves just as much thought.

The baby has arrived. We are here for the mother.
